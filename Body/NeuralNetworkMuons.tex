\chapter{Neural Network Based Reconstructions of a Muon Observable}\label{chap:nn}
The previous chapter highlights the importance of combining radio $X_{\rm max}$ and muon measurements for the prospect of increasing the event-by-event mass separation between primary cosmic-ray mass groups.
At the Pierre Auger Observatory, an event level $X_{\rm max}$ reconstruction using the radio technique is well established, with results from AERA already published~\cite{auger_prd2024_aeraxmax,auger_prl2024_aeraxmax}.
The UMD provides direct muon measurements of air showers on a per-event basis, which in principle can be combined with per-event radio $X_\text{max}$ reconstructions to increase the overall mass sensitivity of Auger, both per-event and for the average mass composition.
While feasible, implementation is difficult due to both geometrical and timeline restrictions for each detector system.
The AERA and UMD footprints are co-located; however, the geometry of UMD scintillator panels is optimal for vertical air-shower reconstructions while the spacing of the AERA radio antennas makes the AERA $X_{\rm max}$ reconstruction more efficient for air showers with $\theta_{\rm zen}\,>\,$\qty{40}{\degree} (see XXX).
Regarding timeline constraints, AERA, recording air showers since 2011, has fulfilled its primary science goal of proving the usefulness of air-shower reconstructions in the \qtyrange{30}{80}{\mega\hertz} band near Malarg\"{u}e and will soon enter the decomissioning phase.
Although the UMD did not obtain a stable reconstruction until January 2018, with deployment only finishing in March 2026, severely limiting the data overlap between the two reconstruction methods.
The radio $X_{\rm max}$ reconstruction will eventually be ported to the RD antennas, deployed at each Auger SD, yet the spacing of RD will incur the same reconstruction efficiency constraint as AERA.

\section{Network Architecture}\label{sec:architecture}
The network architecture was inherited from~\cite{auger_jinst2021_dnnmuons} and is classified as a conditional recurrent neural network (RNN), meaning static features set the initial state and inform the hidden layers of the time-series prediction blocks of the RNN.
The RNN blocks are formed by long short-term memory (LSTM) layers that process information sequentially, predicting the outcome at the present time based on knowledge of the previous time evolution of the dataset.
The static features are first input to several fully connected, i.e. dense, layers to encode their information and are then combined with the LSTM blocks to set the initial conditions of the time evolution.
Finally, the LSTM output is transposed and fed to a final dense layer.
This transpose and final dense layer removes the strict causality requirement of the temporal data from in the LSTM blocks, allowing a bidirectional flow of predictive information across the time series.
A bidirectional LSTM architecture would induce a similar effect.

The input to the network is a vector of length 203, with three static features and one time series input with a length of 200 time bins.
The goal of the conditional RNN is to predict the muonic component of the Auger water-Cherenkov detector PMT traces at the station level, therefore static variables with a high dependence on the air-shower muon number are chosen.
The static variables are the station distance to the air-shower axis ($r$), the azimuthal angle in the shower plane between the station and the incoming air-shower direction ($\psi$), and the secant of the zenith angle ($\sec\theta$).
The end goal is a station level result meaning the inputs should also be station level to remove any inherent event level dependencies leaking into the analysis; however, the use of the zenith angle is unavoidable as the incoming direction of the air shower directly influences the muonic component of the air shower that reaches ground because of attenuation effects.
The time series input is the first 200 times bins of the PMT traces from the Auger water-Cherenkov detector, averaged over all active PMTs for that station.
All analysis will be performed for the Phase I data of the Pierre Auger Observatory, recorded with the UB electronics of the Auger SD.
These 200 bins then correspond to the first \qty{5}{\micro\second} of the \qty{19.2}{\micro\second} PMT trace.
99\% of the muonic component of the signal is recorded within this first 26\% of the trace data~\cite{auger_jinst2021_dnnmuons, juanmiguel_phd2020}.
